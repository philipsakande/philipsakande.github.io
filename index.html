Spiritual Progression — 3.2 Guiding Principles

1. An Eye Single to the Glory of God — Stewardship in Small Things

If my eye is single, my whole body is full of light (3 Nephi 13:22). As Executive Secretary to the Bishopric, I keep minutes, records, and confidential information. No one checks every line, but I check it anyway because stewardship is sacred. As an electrician trained at MOMAS, I learned the same truth in a Lagos 3-bedroom flat where 2.5mm and 1.5mm wires were twisted together without a ferrule. That loose joint heated up and could have burned a home. A loose contact in wiring is like a loose permission in IT or a loose word in a record — small, hidden, and dangerous. As an IT professional, I will not take shortcuts in VLANs, passwords, or backups. As a film editor, I will not cut corners on truth in storytelling.

2. Diligence — Small Things Bring Great Things

“Out of small things proceedeth that which is great” (D&C 64:33-34). Seven years as a Film Editor and Cinematographer did not come from one big job. It came from hours in a quiet editing bay at night — cutting, colour grading, fixing audio, rendering again. My BSc, my NABTEB, my NSQ Level 2, my Ensign IT work all came from small daily acts. In Packet Tracer Project A — Router 4331, Switch 2960, VLAN 10 Sales 192.168.10.0/24, VLAN 20 IT 192.168.20.0/24, Router-on-a-Stick, DHCP, ACL, NAT — I learned one wrong subnet mask breaks the whole network. Diligence means I test before I deploy, document before I forget, and finish what I start.

3. Gather Together in One — Faith and Tech Intertwined Like a Rope

Elder David A. Bednar taught that doctrine, principles, and application must be gathered together in one, like strands intertwined into a rope. My Secretary work, film work, electrical work, and IT work are not four separate lives — they are strands of one rope. Secretary work taught me documentation and confidentiality. Film editing taught me to cut clutter and tell a clear story. Electrical work taught me inspection and safety. IT teaches me to build systems people can trust. I do not want a career separate from my faith. I want technology that gathers, protects, and lifts people.

Project 1 — Multi-Site Secure Network (Packet Tracer) — Abuja, Lagos, Kano — Network Built on Stewardship

Context / Task: Design a secure small-business LAN for a company with Sales and IT teams that must be separated but share internet. Devices: Router 4331, Switch 2960.

Challenge: Sales traffic was leaking into IT, DHCP was misconfigured, and one wrong subnet mask broke connectivity. I had to learn Router-on-a-Stick from scratch.

How I Overcame: Created VLAN 10 Sales 192.168.10.0/24 and VLAN 20 IT 192.168.20.0/24, configured trunk + sub-interfaces, DHCP pools per VLAN, ACL to restrict cross-VLAN access, NAT for internet. Tested with ping and show ip route, fixed ACL order after first test failed.

Result: Sales and IT isolated, both online, ACL enforced, documented config.

Lesson + Spiritual Growth: Like the Lagos flat loose joint, a loose network rule can burn trust. Stewardship means segmenting, testing, and documenting — not just making it “work.” D&C 64:33 — small faithful configs build a great network.

4.2 / 4.3 Refinement — Multi-Site Secure Network (Abuja – Lagos – Kano): I expanded this project to three sites: R1-Abuja, R2-Lagos, R3-Kano, with SW1–SW4, DNS, Web, and RADIUS-Server. I added WPA2-Enterprise wireless on WRT300N. Laptop-1 connects with SSID Default, username students, authenticated by RADIUS-Server. Verified with ping 192.168.0.1 — Reply. I added least-privilege ACLs, logging on ACL denies, a 1-page Acceptable Use / consent note, testing + rollback config, and documentation. Rogue-PC is left unconnected to prove port-security blocks unauthorized devices. From 4.1 I learned from Google location tracking, Apple AirTag, and Uber's breach cover-up that a working tool can still harm people who never consented — so I now design for the person who is not in the room.

Project 2 — Ubuntu Server Hardening — Apache2 403 Fix

Context / Task: Deploy and harden an Ubuntu server running Apache2 for a portfolio / ministry site. Users got 403 Forbidden.

Challenge: Site would not load. I did not know if it was permissions, ownership, or config. Guessing would have made it worse.

How I Overcame: Used journalctl to read Apache logs, checked permissions with ls -l, found www-data ownership wrong. Fixed with chown, chmod, and verified config, then re-tested. Added least-privilege rules and documented the fix.

Result: Site live, secure permissions, logs clean, repeatable fix documented.

Lesson + Spiritual Growth: Inspection is a spiritual discipline. In electrical work I inspect for defects; in IT I inspect logs. Reverence invites revelation — slowing down to read the log is like slowing down to pray: you see what you missed when rushing.

Project 3 — TY KINGS MULTIMEDIA Production & Proforma — Storytelling With Integrity

Context / Task: Produce live video coverage and a professional proforma invoice for JOYCOMETH Ministries — TY KINGS MULTIMEDIA. Client needed a trustworthy quote for equipment and coverage.

Challenge: First quote had wrong totals and unclear specs. Client could have lost trust. Low-light venue also threatened video quality.
