Spiritual Progression — 3.2 Guiding Principles

Portfolio: https://philipsakande.github.io | Scripture: 3 Nephi 13:19-22

1. An Eye Single to the Glory of God — Stewardship in Small Things

If my eye is single, my whole body is full of light (3 Nephi 13:22). As Executive Secretary to the Bishopric, I keep minutes, records, and confidential information. No one checks every line, but I check it anyway because stewardship is sacred. As an electrician trained at MOMAS, I learned the same truth in a Lagos 3-bedroom flat where 2.5mm and 1.5mm wires were twisted together without a ferrule. That loose joint heated up and could have burned a home. A loose contact in wiring is like a loose permission in IT or a loose word in a record — small, hidden, and dangerous. As an IT professional, I will not take shortcuts in VLANs, passwords, or backups. As a film editor, I will not cut corners on truth in storytelling.

2. Diligence — Small Things Bring Great Things

“Out of small things proceedeth that which is great” (D&C 64:33-34). Seven years as a Film Editor and Cinematographer did not come from one big job. It came from hours in a quiet editing bay at night — cutting, colour grading, fixing audio, rendering again. My BSc, my NABTEB, my NSQ Level 2, my Ensign IT work all came from small daily acts. In Packet Tracer Project A — Router 4331, Switch 2960, VLAN 10 Sales 192.168.10.0/24, VLAN 20 IT 192.168.20.0/24, Router-on-a-Stick, DHCP, ACL, NAT — I learned one wrong subnet mask breaks the whole network. Diligence means I test before I deploy, document before I forget, and finish what I start.

3. Gather Together in One — Faith and Tech Intertwined Like a Rope

Elder David A. Bednar taught that doctrine, principles, and application must be gathered together in one, like strands intertwined into a rope. My Secretary work, film work, electrical work, and IT work are not four separate lives — they are strands of one rope. Secretary work taught me documentation and confidentiality. Film editing taught me to cut clutter and tell a clear story. Electrical work taught me inspection and safety. IT teaches me to build systems people can trust. I do not want a career separate from my faith. I want technology that gathers, protects, and lifts people.

Reflection (3.2): My guiding principles are stewardship, diligence, and gathering in one. I test before I deploy, document before I forget, and protect confidentiality — in Bishopric records, in wiring, in film, and in networks.

Project 1 — Multi-Site Secure Network (Packet Tracer) — Abuja, Lagos, Kano

Task: Design a secure small-business LAN for Sales and IT teams that must be separated but share internet. Router 4331, Switch 2960.

Challenge: Sales traffic leaking into IT, DHCP misconfigured, one wrong subnet mask broke connectivity.

Solution: VLAN 10 Sales 192.168.10.0/24, VLAN 20 IT 192.168.20.0/24, Router-on-a-Stick, DHCP pools per VLAN, ACL, NAT. Expanded to 3 sites: R1-Abuja, R2-Lagos, R3-Kano, SW1–SW4, DNS, Web, RADIUS-Server. WPA2-Enterprise on WRT300N. Laptop-1 connects SSID Default, username students, authenticated by RADIUS. Verified ping 192.168.0.1 — Reply. Least-privilege ACLs, logging on denies, Acceptable Use / consent note, rollback config. Rogue-PC left unconnected to prove port-security blocks unauthorized devices.

Lesson: Like the Lagos loose joint, a loose network rule can burn trust. D&C 64:33 — small faithful configs build a great network.

Project 2 — Ubuntu Server Hardening — Apache2 403 Fix

Task: Deploy and harden Ubuntu server running Apache2. Users got 403 Forbidden.

Solution: Used journalctl to read Apache logs, checked permissions with ls -l, found www-data ownership wrong. Fixed with chown, chmod, verified config, re-tested. Added least-privilege rules and documented fix.

Result: Site live, secure permissions, logs clean, repeatable fix documented. Inspection is a spiritual discipline.

Project 3 — TY KINGS MULTIMEDIA Production & Proforma — Storytelling With Integrity

Task: Produce live video coverage and professional proforma invoice for JOYCOMETH Ministries — TY KINGS MULTIMEDIA.

Solution: Corrected total to N6,050,000, specified Sony Cine Large Format Camera and Lenses QTY 8 — N3,750,000, noted “Recommended for Low Light,” rebuilt invoice in Word, planned low-light camera settings.

Result: Client approved, coverage delivered cleanly, invoice trusted. An honest invoice is stewardship.

Read about my cinematography

As a videographer, I plan the shot before I press record: What is the story? Who is the audience? What should they feel? On set I control framing, light, movement, and sound so the footage is clean when it reaches the edit — steady shots, honest colours, and faces that are lit with care. Whether it is a church event, a documentary moment, or a client project, I shoot to serve the story, not to show off the camera.

As a film editor, I do the second half of cinematography: I choose what stays, what goes, and in what order. I cut for pacing and meaning, grade colour for consistency and mood, clean audio, and add motion graphics only where they help understanding. Good editing is invisible — the viewer should remember the message, not the effect.

What connects both roles to my IT work: the same discipline — stewardship, diligence, and an eye single to the glory of God. In the edit room as in systems administration, I check the details, protect the original files, back up the work, and deliver on time. Cinematography taught me to see; IT teaches me to protect what is seen.
